Hi, I’m Mark.
I’ve been in Japan for 20 years.

When I arrived, I couldn’t read a single kana. Looking back, I’m not sure whether that was courage or just not knowing what I was getting into.

I didn’t go to film school. But after this long in Japan, I know better than most how people here work together: what you can’t say outright, and the rules nobody tells you about.

Plenty of people speak good Japanese. Far fewer understand how Japan really works.

That second part is what this job is really about.

In 2024 I worked with Discovery on the medical documentary Medical Elite: Regenerative Medicine. To be honest, that was the first time I’d heard the word “fixer.” By the end of the shoot, the director joked that I’d make a good assistant director.

I love seeing something through with a crew. Every wrap is a little sad, though: I catch myself thinking I may never see these people again. That’s why every project matters to me.

On a shoot, things rarely go to plan. The weather turns, the schedule slips, a location suddenly falls through.

Staying calm and having the next option ready is what I’m good at. When the plan stops working, that’s when I step in.

FAQ

Which languages do you work in?

Chinese and Japanese. On-site interpreting is Chinese ⇄ Japanese, and I carry over the director’s intent and the interviewee’s tone, not just the words.

How are you different from a local production company?

I handle everything myself, from early negotiations and permits to on-site interpreting. Anyone can follow a plan. My strength is when the plan stops working: the weather turns, the schedule slips, a location falls through, and the next option has to be ready.

Can you get access to hospitals, research labs or factories?

I can negotiate access. I took the Discovery crew into regenerative-medicine labs and clean rooms, and arranged filming and interviews inside a pharmaceutical factory for furrmacy. Whether filming is allowed is up to the host, so the earlier we start, the better.

How much does it cost?

Each project is quoted individually, based on what you’re filming, how many days, where, and what needs arranging. Send me your plan, dates and locations and I’ll reply with an estimate.

How far in advance should I get in touch?

As early as you can. Permits and interview access take time, especially with hospitals and companies. If your dates aren’t fixed yet, that’s fine: get in touch and we can talk through what’s feasible.

Who have you worked with?

Discovery on the medical documentary Medical Elite: Regenerative Medicine (Finalist, New York Festivals 2026, Documentary), Singapore’s Mediacorp on the outdoor series Camping in the Corners of the World, and furrmacy on its UAE promotional film.
